      <p class="ip-p bp__p">If your readiness score puts you in the borderline zone, the good news is that it's usually the fastest gap to close — you don't need to relearn your fundamentals, you need a focused eight weeks. This is the plan we've seen work most consistently.</p>

      <h2 class="ip-h2" id="diagnose">Weeks 1–2: Diagnose, don't guess</h2>
      <p class="ip-p bp__p">Before you study anything, find out exactly which category is dragging your score down — communication, aptitude, or domain depth. Most students assume it's domain knowledge when it's actually communication, and spend two months on the wrong thing.</p>
      <h2 class="ip-h2" id="one-gap">Weeks 3–5: One gap at a time</h2>
      <p class="ip-p bp__p">Resist the urge to fix everything at once. Pick the lowest-scoring category and drill it daily for three weeks before moving to the next. Splitting attention across three weak areas at once is why most last-minute prep doesn't move the needle.</p>
      <ul class="bp__list">
        <li>Communication: record yourself answering common interview questions and review the playback — most people are surprised by their own filler words.</li>
        <li>Aptitude: timed daily practice sets beat occasional long sessions. Consistency matters more than volume.</li>
        <li>Domain: pick 3–4 likely interview topics from your target JD and go deep, rather than reviewing your entire syllabus shallowly.</li>
      </ul>
      <div class="bp__quote">Borderline isn't a talent problem. It's an eight-week problem, if you fix the right thing first.</div>
      <h2 class="ip-h2" id="mocks">Weeks 6–8: Mock interviews, then re-score</h2>
      <p class="ip-p bp__p">Run at least four mock interviews in the final two weeks, with feedback after each one. Re-check your readiness score in week 8 — if it's crossed the threshold, shift fully into interview prep. If it hasn't, you now know exactly which category still needs work, instead of guessing again.</p>
      <p class="ip-p bp__p">Of the 200 students who followed a version of this plan, the ones who diagnosed correctly in week 1 closed their gap nearly twice as fast as those who started studying before finding out what was actually wrong.</p>
